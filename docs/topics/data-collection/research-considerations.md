# Research Considerations

Read this page before you start a collection. The platform's rules, the content you will be exposed to, and the people behind the posts all shape what you should collect and how.

## Terms of service

Most platforms publish rules for API use. Read them before collecting, and prefer the official API to scraping. What the rules usually say, using [Bluesky's developer guidelines](https://docs.bsky.app/docs/support/developer-guidelines) as the example:

- No spam: no automated or bulk interactions such as follows, likes, or replies. Reading is fine; interacting from a script is not
- No automated account creation
- Protect the user data you hold; delete what a user asks you to delete
- Violations "may result in suspension of access"

Researchers disagree about how much weight the terms deserve. The case for following them: researchers should behave ethically and keep their access. The case against: the terms are not laws, they can block research that serves the public, and researchers have a larger responsibility to the collective good than to the platforms. Whatever you decide, there can be repercussions. Two examples:

- **X Corp. v. Center for Countering Digital Hate.** X sued CCDH in 2023 over research on hate speech on the platform, claiming that the automated collection of public data violated its terms of service and cost X advertising revenue. The district court dismissed the case on March 25, 2024, and X appealed. [Case page at the Knight First Amendment Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/cases/x-corp-v-center-for-countering-digital-hate).
- **Records requests to universities.** In February 2025 a legal advocacy group requested ten types of records from Indiana University over alleged participation in a "censorship-industrial complex", one of several such requests to universities that host misinformation research. [Report in the Indiana Daily Student](https://www.idsnews.com/article/2025/02/iu-conservative-christian-investigation-adf-censorship).

## Beyond the terms of service

- **Public is not the same as consented.** People post for their followers, not for your dataset.
- **Collect what the question needs**, not everything the API will give you.
- **Do not try to identify people.** Anonymous posts still describe real people. Do not republish posts with identifying details, and do not collect private content.
- **Deleted content stays deleted.** If a user removes a post, drop it from what you publish.
- **Ethics review.** Ask your institution's review board whether a study of public social media data needs review before you collect, not after. Public social media data is often exempt, but the board decides that, not you.

## Offensive content

4chan boards, and parts of every platform, contain material that is offensive, hateful, or not safe for work. Decide in advance which boards or communities the research question needs, and collect only those. Think about your own exposure and that of anyone who will read the data.

## Images and other media

Post records carry file names or URLs, not files. Download images and videos only when the research question needs them, and expect the URLs to expire.
